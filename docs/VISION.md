# Vision

## The one-liner
Wikipedia's openness + an argument map's structure + a "follow the money" graph — laid out as a 3D river of time you can fly through.

## The experience
1. **The River.** Human history as a glowing river in 3D. Parallel strands (lanes) for fields: politics, science, medicine, economics, culture, technology. Zoom from millennia down to a single day.
2. **Events.** Glowing nodes on the river. "1 Sep 1939 — WW2 begins in Europe."
3. **Dive in → Claim Map.** Entering an event opens a mind-map. The event sits at the centre; bubbles fork off for topics and debates:
   - *"Was Churchill a hero or a villain?"*
   - *"Did the UK 'win' WW2 if it lost its empire?"* → which itself forks into *"Yes, the empire ended"*, *"Victory was military, decline was economic"*, *"Decline began before 1939"* …
   Each fork is a **theory → claim → evidence → counter-claim** chain. It shows that things are more complicated than one headline, while still letting simple true statements ("the empire did end") stand clearly.
4. **Every node shows provenance.** Who said it, which source, the author, their institution, and **who funded them** — with a trust score you can expand to see exactly why.
5. **Rabbit holes.** Click a funder → warp to its founding on the river → see everything else it funded → keep going. Trails are saved and shareable.
6. **Change over time.** Every claim and topic has a history graph: how evidence, status and public opinion have swung, with correlated events marked on the same chart.
7. **"What does AI think?"** Any claim can carry timestamped AI snapshots: which model, when, the exact prompts, the output — and whether other users reproduced it. Over years this becomes a record of how AI shaped public knowledge.
8. **The town square.** Anyone can post a claim, including alternate views and conspiracy theories. They get the full treatment: strongest version (steelman), cited evidence, counter-evidence, and "what would prove this wrong?". Bad ideas are judged in the open by the community, not hidden by a few.

## Desktop vs mobile
- **Desktop:** free-flying 3D river, hover previews, side panels.
- **Mobile:** a vertical "dive" tunnel — swipe/tilt through time, tap to enter maps. Still 3D, built for thumbs. Same data, same links.

## What makes it different
- Funding and penalties (fines, settlements, retractions) are **first-class data**, not footnotes.
- Labels are **computed and explainable**, not decided by staff.
- **Public opinion is data too** — tracked over time, not used to decide truth.
- The platform's **own money is on the timeline**.
